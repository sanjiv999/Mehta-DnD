# Chapter 3: The Mirror Fort

## Premise
Three days west, past the last well, there is a ruined caravanserai that a rakshasa rebuilt by
setting every outer wall with mirror pieces. At noon it cannot be looked at. At sunset it shows
you yourself. Dhoomketu keeps the jars of stolen colour there because the Vizier came once,
twenty years ago, said "magnificent", and left. He has painted four hundred portraits of himself
waiting. The chapter is a journey, a door that is not there, and a boss who would rather be
reviewed than fought.

## Goal the party will understand
"Get to the fort, get the colours back, and find out who the rakshasa is keeping them for."

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Eleven camels (travel)
> The west gate is where the city stops being red. Beyond it, thorn scrub and a dirt road with
> a line of camels kneeling on it, and a woman in faded indigo checking each girth strap by
> hand. There are eleven camels and she has eleven tassels on her belt, one for each. "Three
> days," she says, "if the wind is kind. Four if it is not. Pick a camel. The camel also picks."
- **Setup:** Dawn, the day after chapter 2. Gulnar. Three days of travel; roll the desert table in `dm/encounters.md` each day. Each hero names a camel and that name is canon. Water discipline is real: Gulnar rations by cup.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Name your camel. Keshu rolls the weather die each morning. Aarya reads Fariduddin's astrolabe at night; it points at the nearest lie, and on the second night it points at the fort.
- **Choices:**
  - 🐪 The fast road (two days, two encounters, no oasis)
  - 🌴 The oasis road (three days, one encounter, a yaksha who has lost his coin)
  - 🌙 By night (cool, stars, one strange encounter, the camels prefer it)
- **Rolls:** CON save Tricky in a sandstorm. Animals Easy to calm a camel that has decided to sit. The yaksha's riddle answer is "shadow"; it is the key to scene 3.
- **Outcomes:** Salt traders coming the other way say the fort has started "singing at noon" this year. Gulnar does not like that at all.
- **Image:** A caravan of eleven camels leaving a red gate at dawn, thorn scrub, a woman in indigo walking beside the lead camel checking a strap
- **Music:** camel bells, wind, one string

### Scene 2: The wall with no door (puzzle)
> At noon it is a flash on the horizon that the camels will not walk toward, so you wait. At
> sunset it is a square fort on a dune, and every wall is set with pieces of mirror the size of
> your palm, thousands of them, and in every piece there is a small you. There is no gate on
> any side. Gulnar shrugs. "People try to go in. They walk into the wall and stand there."
> On the north wall, your reflection raises a hand. You did not.
- **Setup:** Entry: each hero steps into their own reflection and says one true thing about themselves. A false thing bounces. Inside, three corridors of mirror; only the one where your reflection has a shadow is real (the yaksha's answer).
- **Jobs for the kids:** Say one true thing ("I am scared of thunder"; "I love my sister"). Cannot fail; the reflection steps aside. Aarya looks for the shadow.
- **Choices:**
  - 🪞 Step into your reflection
  - 🔨 Hit the glass (it shows you crying; nothing else happens)
  - 🐪 Send a camel first (its reflection declines)
- **Outcomes:** Wrong corridors meet a **Reflection** (dm/bestiary.md), a grey copy with 5 HP that steps back into the glass when the hero says something true about themselves. Three black mirror pieces on the north wall are where the Vizier has been watching.
- **Image:** Four small figures facing a wall of thousands of mirror pieces at sunset, one reflection among them with its hand raised
- **Music:** glass ticking as it cools

### Scene 3: Four hundred portraits (social)
> The hall in the middle is the only room with colour in it. Jars on shelves to the ceiling,
> each one lit from inside: the red of a ruby, the green of a parrot, the exact grey-blue of
> the baoli's water before it went grey. Portraits of the same tiger-faced figure cover every
> wall, and he is sitting in the middle of the room painting another one. He does not turn
> round. "Visitors," he says. "Tell me honestly. Am I magnificent?"
- **Setup:** Dhoomketu can be fought (HP 40, three illusions, only the one with a shadow is real) or won. Winning takes three sincere compliments and one honest criticism, and the criticism is the one that lands; nobody has told him the truth in twenty years. A lie makes him grow another head, each more offended.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Each kid gives one real compliment (he can tell). Keshu picks which jar is the baoli's blue; he is right. Aarya tells him something about the painting that is actually wrong with it.
- **Choices:**
  - 🎭 Compliment him, sincerely
  - 🖼️ Tell him what is wrong with the painting
  - 🗡️ Fight
  - 🤥 Flatter him (do not)
- **Rolls:** Persuasion Tricky per compliment; automatic if the player says something specific and true. Fight: see bestiary.
- **Outcomes:** Won over, he admits the Vizier never came back, and gives three jars (one restores one gem). He asks, formally, to accompany the party to Sikri "for an audience." He has been waiting twenty years for someone to leave with.
- **Image:** A tiger-faced rakshasa in silks painting his own portrait in a hall whose walls are covered with portraits of him and whose shelves hold hundreds of lit glass jars
- **Music:** a single sitar flourish, then nothing while he waits

### Scene 4: The mirrors go black (action)
> The moment the first jar leaves its shelf, every mirror in the fort turns black at once, like
> a lamp blown out in a room full of windows. Then, in every black pane, the same face: the
> Vizier, smiling, the way he smiles at the Padishah. "Friends of the Throne. So far from home."
> The black glass cracks. Something grey climbs out of every crack.
- **Setup:** Escape. Mirror-Shards come through the walls, one per hero per round for three rounds. The ceiling is coming down as sand. Dhoomketu knows the back way: a mirror in his own bedroom that reflects the baoli in Sikri, which he has never used because he had nowhere to go.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keshu carries the blue jar in both arms and does not drop it (he will not). Aarya counts the rounds aloud; on three, Dhoomketu's mirror opens.
- **Choices:**
  - 🏃 Run with Dhoomketu
  - 🛡️ Hold the hall while the jars are passed hand to hand
  - 🌈 Open a jar at the shards (2d8 to all of them; the jar is spent)
- **Rolls:** DEX saves Tricky for falling glass. Attacks as normal.
- **Outcomes:** The party steps through a bedroom mirror and out onto the dry step of the baoli, three days away, with sand in their hair. Lady Shabnam is standing there. When the blue jar is opened over the water, one square of it goes the right colour. Firoz asks if he can come properly now.
- **Image:** Four heroes running down a corridor of cracking black mirrors carrying lit glass jars, a tiger-faced figure leading, sand pouring from the ceiling
- **Music:** drums, breaking glass, then water dripping

## Complications (roll d6)
1. Sandstorm buries the camp. Dig the camels out, Athletics Easy, everyone.
2. A sand djinn-let takes a kid's shoe and settles into a sandcastle the shape of the kid. The shoe is on the top.
3. A mirage of your own front door. WIS Easy or you lose an hour explaining to a mirage parent where you have been.
4. Gulnar's lead camel will not stand until someone sings. Badly is fine.
5. A Reflection of Zeb appears in the maze and says the party is being followed. It is right.
6. Dhoomketu sneezes and two heroes have each other's voices for a scene.

## Rewards
- 250 XP each. Level 4.
- Three jars of colour. Dhoomketu as an ally. Firoz as a companion (5 HP, trick: one thing, one colour).
- A camel each, in theory.

## How it ends and what it sets up
One gem is restored in front of the court, and the Padishah laughs for the first time in a week.
The Vizier congratulates the heroes in front of everyone. That night the monsoon, which is due,
does not come, and in the morning the river is running the wrong way.
