# Chapter 5: The Peacock's Last Feather

## Premise
Qamar's plan is simple and he believes it is fair. A throne with no colour belongs to whoever
holds the mirror, and a dyer's son will sit on it where his father was not allowed to hand over
a bolt of cloth. Tonight, at the full-moon audience, he raises the mirror to the last gem. The
heroes have one day to gather their friends and one night to show him the thing he has had
wrong since he was nine.

## Goal the party will understand
"Stop the Vizier before moonrise, light the last gem, and get the Padishah's laugh back."

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Everyone you have met (montage)
> Bibi Chandni closes her stall. She has not closed it in thirty-one years and the whole street
> comes out to watch her do it. On the roofs, the runners are going out with notes. A palanquin
> painted on every surface with the same tiger's face is coming up the hill from the west gate.
> Rukmini ties her boat to the palace steps. On the fifth floor of the Panch Mahal, an old man
> unlocks a drawer he has kept locked for twenty years.
- **Setup:** Each ally the party earned offers one thing for tonight (table in dm/secrets.md). Fariduddin's drawer holds Qamar's first mirror. Daulat, if treated kindly, brings a child's drawing of a blue peacock. Three allies can come into the hall; the rest wait outside.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Choose the three. Keisha hands out the Wish Lamps. Arya asks Mirza for his word; he says "feather."
- **Choices:**
  - 🐒 Hanuvant's runners (a way out through the roof)
  - 🐯 Dhoomketu (counter-illusions)
  - 🐍 Tara or Padma's envoy (the sky-jar)
  - 🐘 Badal (a wall, and he hears the true path)
  - 🔭 Fariduddin and the first mirror
  - 🪞 Daulat and the drawing
- **Outcomes:** A plan. Three allies. The full moon rising over the lake.
- **Image:** Allies arriving at red palace steps at dusk, a langur captain, a tiger-faced rakshasa's painted palanquin, a boatwoman tying off, an old astronomer with a small mirror
- **Music:** strings, gathering

### Scene 2: The hall of mirrors (action)
> The Hall of Audience is wrong. Mirrors hang between every pillar and the hall has become a
> maze of yourselves, and somewhere in it the Padishah's voice, small: "Zeb? I can't find the
> throne." Something is pacing between the mirrors. A feather, green and blue, grown the height
> of a tree, with an eye in it.
- **Setup:** The **Mirror-Maze Guardian** (dm/bestiary.md) is Mirza's missing tail feather. It can only be hit by a hero looking away from it, into a reflection. Returned to Mirza, it shrinks and he gets his voice.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keisha holds a Wish Lamp up so everyone can see which reflections have shadows. Arya follows the Padishah's voice and brings him back by the hand (cannot fail).
- **Choices:**
  - 🪶 Catch the feather and give it to Mirza
  - 🗡️ Fight it head on (disadvantage)
  - 🐘 Let Badal hum; he walks the real path and you follow
  - 🔑 Wind the Clockwork Peacock with the key; it dances, and the mirrors fall
- **Rolls:** Attacks via reflection at normal odds, direct at disadvantage. Perception Tricky to find the shadowed path without Badal.
- **Outcomes:** The feather returns to Mirza. He clears his throat. Everyone turns round.
- **Image:** A pillared red hall turned into a maze of tall mirrors, a giant iridescent feather with an eye pacing between them, a boy's hand reaching from behind a pillar
- **Music:** tense strings, glass

### Scene 3: What the parrot heard (revelation)
> Mirza flies to the top of the throne and speaks for the first time in a year, in the voice of
> an old teacher. "Twenty years ago, in this hall, with nobody in it but me, a young man said he
> would own this throne by owning its colours, because colour was the only thing that made a
> person count. Qamar. You were wrong then." A pause. "Shall I tell them what else you said?"
> The Vizier's mirror, for the first time, is turned toward himself.
- **Setup:** The court is listening. The Vizier is exposed and not beaten. This is the players' chance to understand him before the last scene: the dyers' quarter, his father's hands, the bolt of cloth nobody would take.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Ask the Vizier one question each. If he will not answer, Mirza will.
- **Choices:**
  - ❓ Ask him why
  - 😠 Tell him what he did to the Padishah
  - 🤲 Offer him a colour of his own
  - 🪞 Ask Daulat to step forward
- **Outcomes:** Whatever is said, he raises the mirror. The last gem starts to go.
- **Image:** A green parrot on top of a golden throne, speaking, a man in grey silk below holding a small mirror he is for once looking into, a court of hundreds silent
- **Music:** one sitar note

### Scene 4: True colours (climax)
> The mirror drinks the room. The colour runs out of the banners and the carpets and the court's
> faces and into the glass in his hand, and the Padishah's turban goes grey, and Zeb's ink, and
> the marigolds on Badal. The only colour left in the hall is standing where you are standing.
> "Show me," says the Vizier, and it is not an order. "Show me what counts, if this does not."
- **Setup:** Each hero shows him one true colour: a value, a person, a thing they did. The player says it; then rolls Persuasion, Performance or any ability the DM allows, Tricky. Each success cracks the mirror. Four cracks and it goes. A failure costs a Hero Point or 1d8 of greying (HP). Allies give advantage. Fariduddin's first mirror, Padma's Pearl, the sky-jar, a Wish Lamp, the winding key and Daulat's drawing each give one automatic crack if used with sense.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Say your colour out loud. The DM narrates each crack as that colour comes back into the room, Keisha's first.
- **Choices:**
  - 💛 Show him joy
  - ❤️ Show him love
  - 💙 Show him honesty
  - 💚 Show him courage
  - 🪞 Hold up the first mirror
  - 🌈 Pour the sky-jar on the throne
  - 🖼️ Give him the drawing
- **Rolls:** As above. The mirror has four "cracks" of HP.
- **Outcomes:** The mirror breaks into coloured sand. The colour comes back into everything at once. The central feather of the throne opens and inside it is a wick that does not burn down. The Padishah laughs so hard he slides off the throne. Then the choice.
- **Image:** A hand mirror breaking into a storm of coloured sand in a grey hall, colour flooding back from four small heroes standing together
- **Music:** everything, and drums

### Scene 5: A dyer's son (epilogue)
> He is kneeling in the sand and he is grey all over, like a drawing someone stopped before they
> got to the colours. His brother is standing beside him with blue arms, holding out a piece of
> paper. Zeb looks at you. The Padishah looks at you. The parrot tilts his head. "Well," says
> Mirza. "What shall we do with him?"
- **Setup:** The party decides. If two or more choose mercy, Qamar becomes keeper of the Baoli's Door, grey-robed, and the first thing he does is go down to the vats. Exile or prison are allowed and narrated gently.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Vote. Then choose Badal's medal.
- **Choices:**
  - 🕊️ Forgive him and give him a job
  - 🚪 Send him away
  - 🔒 Lock him up
  - 🎨 Give him one colour back
- **Outcomes:** The dry step of the baoli opens. Lady Shabnam stands in it. The lake rises a step overnight. `python tools/campaign.py complete peacock-throne`.
- **Image:** A grey kneeling man in a storm of coloured sand, a blue-armed dyer beside him holding out a child's drawing, a small hero offering a jar of colour
- **Music:** quiet

## Complications (roll d6)
1. Dhoomketu gets stage fright and sends an illusion of himself to do the talking. It is better at it.
2. The Padishah tries to help and gets into a mirror. Arya pulls him out by the turban.
3. The Clockwork Peacock, wound, will not stop dancing. The Vizier cannot concentrate. Good.
4. The runners open the roof and the full moon comes in and every mirror goes white.
5. Tara sneezes and it rains inside the hall for a round. Everyone slips, including the Vizier.
6. Bibi Chandni throws a laddoo. It hits. He nearly smiles.

## Rewards
- 500 XP each. The Lantern's wick. "Hero of the Peacock Throne" for everyone.
- Every ally's farewell gift (dm/secrets.md). Badal: a medal. Firoz: a colour of his own.

## How it ends and what it sets up
The Door is open. The party may cross with the wick, carrying one keepsake each. The next
world's Door is waiting on the other side of the dry step.
