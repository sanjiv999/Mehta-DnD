# Chapter 5: The Peacock's Last Feather

## Premise
One gem left. At tonight's full-moon audience the Vizier will raise his mirror to the throne
and claim it: a throne with no colour can be ruled by whoever holds the mirror. The heroes have
a day to prepare, allies to gather, and then a finale in front of everyone they have met.

## Goal the party will understand
"Stop the Vizier before moonrise, restore the last gem, and save the Padishah's smile."

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Gathering the friends (social, montage)
> Word goes out across the city. Bibi Chandni closes her stall for the first time in thirty
> years. Hanuvant's runners carry notes to every rooftop. Dhoomketu arrives in a palanquin he
> painted himself. Rukmini ties her boat to the palace steps. Hakim Fariduddin unlocks a drawer
> he has kept shut for twenty years and takes out a small, old mirror.
- **Setup:** Montage. Each ally the party helped earlier offers something for tonight (see dm/secrets.md for the list). Fariduddin's drawer holds the Vizier's first mirror, which can crack the big one.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Pick which three allies to bring into the throne room (the rest wait outside). Keisha hands out the Wish Lamps. Arya asks Mirza to say one word; he says "feather".
- **Choices:**
  - 🐒 Bring Hanuvant's runners (rooftop escape route)
  - 🐯 Bring Dhoomketu (counter-illusions)
  - 🐍 Bring Nagini Tara or Padma's envoy (the sky-jar)
  - 🐘 Bring Badal (a wall to hide behind, and he can hear colour)
  - 🔭 Bring Fariduddin and the old mirror
- **Outcomes:** The party enters the audience with three allies and a plan.
- **Image:** A montage of allies arriving at marble palace steps at dusk: a monkey captain, a tiger-faced rakshasa in a painted palanquin, a boatwoman, an old astronomer
- **Music:** gathering strings, hopeful

### Scene 2: The mirror maze (exploration, action)
> The throne room is wrong. The Vizier has hung mirrors on every pillar, and the hall has become
> a maze of yourselves. Somewhere ahead, the Padishah's small voice: "Zeb? Zeb, I can't find the
> throne." And something huge and glittering is pacing between the mirrors: a feather, grown to
> the size of a tree, with eyes.
- **Setup:** The **Mirror-Maze Guardian** (dm/bestiary.md) is Mirza's missing tail feather. It can only be hit by a hero looking away from it (via a reflection). Returned to Mirza, it shrinks and Mirza's voice comes back.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keisha holds a Wish Lamp up so the heroes can see which reflection has a shadow. Arya finds the Padishah by following his voice (cannot fail) and brings him to the party.
- **Choices:**
  - 🪶 Catch the feather and return it to Mirza
  - 🗡️ Fight it directly (disadvantage; the mirrors confuse you)
  - 🐘 Have Badal hum; he hears the real path through the maze
  - 🔑 If someone kept the winding key: rewind the Clockwork Peacock, which dances and the mirrors fall
- **Rolls:** Attacks via reflection at normal odds; direct at disadvantage. Perception Tricky to find the shadowed path without Badal.
- **Outcomes:** The feather returns to Mirza. He clears his throat. He has a lot to say.
- **Image:** A marble hall turned into a maze of tall mirrors, a giant iridescent feather with eyes pacing between them, a small boy's hand reaching from behind a pillar
- **Music:** tense strings, mirror chimes

### Scene 3: What the parrot said (social, revelation)
> Mirza flies to the top of the throne, fluffs up, and speaks for the first time in a year, in a
> voice like an old teacher. "Twenty years ago, in this room, a poor boy told me he would own the
> throne by owning its colours, because colour was the only thing that made a person matter.
> Qamar. You were wrong then. Shall I tell them what else you said?"
> The Vizier's smile, for the first time, is not symmetrical.
- **Setup:** The court is listening. The Vizier is exposed but not beaten; he raises the mirror. This scene is the players' chance to understand him before the fight.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Ask the Vizier one question each. Mirza answers if the Vizier will not.
- **Choices:**
  - ❓ Ask him why
  - 😠 Tell him he hurt the Padishah
  - 🤲 Offer him a colour of his own
- **Outcomes:** Whatever is said, he raises the mirror. The last gem starts to grey. Scene 4.
- **Image:** An emerald parrot on top of a jewelled throne speaking, a silver-robed vizier below with a cracked smile, a court of hundreds listening
- **Music:** silence, one sitar note

### Scene 4: True colours (climax)
> The mirror drinks the room. Colour pours out of the banners, the carpets, the court's faces,
> all of it streaming into the glass in the Vizier's hand. The Padishah's turban goes grey. Zeb's
> ink goes grey. The only colour left in the whole hall is you.
> "Show me," says the Vizier, and it is almost a plea. "Show me what matters, if not this."
- **Setup:** The finale is a roleplay-then-roll for each hero. Each hero shows the Vizier their truest colour: a value (courage, kindness, honesty, joy, love for a sister). The player says it; then rolls Persuasion, Performance, or any ability the DM allows, Tricky. Each success cracks the mirror. Four cracks and it shatters. Failures cost a Hero Point or 1d8 of "greying" (HP). Allies can give advantage. Fariduddin's old mirror, Padma's Pearl, the sky-jar, the Wish Lamps and the winding key each give one automatic crack if used cleverly.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Say their true colour out loud. The DM narrates each crack with that colour flooding back into the room (Keisha's colour first, always).
- **Choices:**
  - 💛 Show him joy
  - ❤️ Show him love
  - 💙 Show him honesty
  - 💚 Show him courage
  - 🪞 Use Fariduddin's old mirror
  - 🌈 Pour the sky-jar over the throne
- **Rolls:** As above. The mirror has, in effect, 4 "cracks" of HP.
- **Outcomes:** The mirror shatters into coloured sand. Colour floods back into everything, the throne blazes like a peacock's tail, and the central feather opens to reveal the **wick of the Lantern of Many Roads**. The Padishah laughs so hard he falls off the throne. Then: the mercy choice.
- **Image:** A hand mirror shattering into a storm of coloured sand in a grey throne room as colour floods back from four small glowing heroes
- **Music:** full orchestra, drums, shehnai, laughter

### Scene 5: Mercy (social, epilogue)
> The Vizier kneels in the coloured sand. He is grey himself now, every bit of him, like a
> drawing someone forgot to finish. Zeb looks at you. The Padishah looks at you. Mirza looks at
> you, head tilted. "Well," says the parrot. "What shall we do with him?"
- **Setup:** The party decides. Mercy (two or more heroes choose it) makes him Keeper of the Baoli's Door, grey-robed, helping heroes cross between worlds. Exile or prison are allowed; the DM narrates them gently.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Vote. Then choose Badal's medal.
- **Choices:**
  - 🕊️ Forgive him and give him a job
  - 🚪 Send him away from the city
  - 🔒 Lock him up
  - 🎨 Give him one colour back
- **Outcomes:** The Baoli's dry step opens into a Door. Lady Shabnam stands in it. The wick glows. Badal gets a medal. The campaign is complete; `python tools/campaign.py complete peacock-throne`.
- **Image:** A grey kneeling vizier in a storm of coloured sand, a small hero holding out a single jar of colour to him
- **Music:** gentle, resolving

## Complications (roll d6)
1. Dhoomketu gets stage fright and creates an illusion of himself to do the talking.
2. The Padishah tries to help and gets stuck in a mirror. Arya pulls him out.
3. The Clockwork Peacock, re-wound, will not stop dancing. It is distracting the Vizier. Good.
4. Hanuvant's runners open the roof; moonlight pours in and the mirrors go bright.
5. Nagini Tara sneezes and the throne room fills with rain for a round. Everyone slips.
6. Bibi Chandni throws a laddoo at the Vizier. It hits. He almost smiles.

## Rewards
- 500 XP each. The Lantern wick. "Hero of the Peacock Throne" achievement for everyone.
- Every ally: a farewell gift (dm/secrets.md). Badal: a medal. Firoz: a colour of his own.

## How it ends and what it sets up
The Door opens. The party may cross to another world with the Lantern wick, carrying one
keepsake each. The next campaign's Door is waiting on the other side.
