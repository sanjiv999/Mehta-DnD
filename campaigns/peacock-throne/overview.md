# The Peacock Throne

## The pitch (read this to the players)
Sikri is a red city on a ridge. Red walls, red gates, a red palace with five storeys of
pillars that hold up only shade. The one white building is the tomb of a saint, where people
tie threads through the marble screens to ask for things. On the first morning of the new year
the whole city is in the streets, because the Padishah is about to be weighed against sacks of
grain and give the grain away, and because at sunset the elephants walk.

The Padishah is eleven. His sister is fourteen and runs the palace while he plays pachisi on a
board cut into the courtyard, with servants for pieces. At the centre of the Hall of Audience is
a throne set with a thousand gems. Last night, one of them went grey.

Also, the Padishah's elephant has walked out of the stable, and the guards let him, because he
bowed to them on the way through.

## The world in five facts
1. The city drinks from a lake that is a little lower every summer. Water-carriers sell it by the cup and go into every house, which is why the sweet-seller at the bazaar mouth knows everything.
2. Colour is water-luck. A grey thing is a thing the water has left.
3. The court keeps cheetahs with ranks and daily meat rations, elephants with names and a register, and parrots because they repeat what they hear.
4. Langurs carry the mail across the rooftops for mangoes. They are not pets and are offended by the suggestion.
5. The djinn of the stepwell made a bargain with the first Padishah. Nobody alive remembers the terms.

## What heroes do here
Find an elephant. Then find out why the throne is losing its colours, across the festival of
lamps on the lake, a fort of mirrors in the western desert, a river that runs uphill in the
monsoon, and the throne room itself.

## Hooks for each hero
- A **kid hero** is landed on by a green parrot with a red collar, which refuses to leave her shoulder and says nothing at all.
- A **warrior or protector** is pulled aside by a stable boy who has been told to find "someone who looks strong and is not from here."
- A **clever hero** notices the edge of her own keepsake has gone grey since she walked through the city gate.
- A **charming hero** has been given a jalebi "on credit" by a woman who then wrote his name in a ledger.
