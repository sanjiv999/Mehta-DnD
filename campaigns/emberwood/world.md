# The Emberwood: world bible

Player-safe except the final section.

## Hearth, as a place to stand in

Hearth is forty houses, a church with a bell that is the only clock, a mill on the stream, an
alehouse with a bush hung over its door to show the ale is ready, and a communal bread oven in
the square that is lit on Tuesdays. Everyone brings their dough to the oven on bake-day and
waits for it talking, which is how news moves. The baker is paid in a loaf from every batch.
The town is fenced by a hedge taller than a house, grown over three hundred years, with stiles
over it in four places and one hollow oak in it with a door.

Beyond the hedge is the Emberwood. The trees glow faintly after dark, like coals under ash, and
have done since before anyone's grandmother. Charcoal burners work in the wood in clearings,
building clamps of logs under turf that smoulder for five days and have to be watched day and
night so they do not catch, which is why charcoal burners sleep in huts beside them and are the
only people who know the wood at night. Goblins live in the wood too, and come to the hedge to
sell.

## What people do

Bread, pottage, cheese, ale. Bacon at Christmas. Pies for the dragon on the first bake-day of
spring: forty of them, apple and pork, baked in the communal oven and carried up the mountain
in a cart with the whole town behind it. This is the rent. The dragon has never once come down,
and the pies have come back untouched this year.

Market day is Saturday: timber stalls in the square, the goblins at the hedge end selling
glowing mushrooms, bottled fog, and boots. The reeve, who is called the mayor because he likes
it, keeps the ledger of who owes what. The council meets in the alehouse and the baker's cat
sits on the table and is counted.

The church bell rings the hours and the alarm. There is no other clock in Hearth and nobody
has felt the lack.

## Goblins

Chapmen: pedlars with packs, who walk the wood's paths because the paths do not move for
goblins, and who hold a market under the big trees on the dark of the moon. They sell what the
wood makes and what the dwarves left. Their prices are terrible. They value a story at ten
silver and a child's drawing at twenty. A goblin will not steal, because the whole market would
know by morning.

## The dwarves and the Ember

The Deep Halls run under the wood and the mountain: a city of pillars carved like trees, forges,
a library of stone, a nursery, and at the bottom the Heart, a memory-stone that held the forge-
fire asleep as long as the dwarves were remembered. The forge-fire was a spirit; the dwarves
called it Kindle, fed it, and talked to it. Three hundred years ago it began copying them: a
dwarf would turn and find a dwarf of coals turning with him. The thane's wife said "don't let it
remember you" and the dwarves left in a night, carrying what they could, through the gate the
goblins now hold their market on. Brokk the thane stayed so the Heart would have one memory to
run on. They were forgotten. The Heart went dark. Kindle has been lonely since, and lonely fire
becomes whatever it sees.

Sunder, the dragon, was a hatchling then and the dwarves' friend. She has lain across the
deepest crack since, keeping the Ember asleep with her own warmth, and the pies are how the town
has thanked her without knowing what for. She cannot leave the crack to eat now.

## The people (depth)

**Councillor Biscuit.** The baker's cat, ginger, fat, with a ribbon the council voted him. Has
always been able to talk and no one asked. Keeper of the oak Door. Wrote the four notes on the
board with a paw. Wants: the pies to work and somebody else to do the walking. Opinion: the
mayor bakes better than the baker and must never be told.

**Mayor Bramble.** The reeve. Round, a cardigan, a chain of office, a clipboard. Bakes the
dragon's pies himself and is proud. Wants: everything to be fine. Fear: the hedge. Follows the
party into it anyway.

**Grizzle.** Goblin chapman in a tweed waistcoat with a pack of glowing mushrooms, which have
started glowing brighter and frighten his customers. Wants a fair price and to stop being
frightened. Fear: the mushrooms. Hands: counts coins twice. Opinion: thinks Nana Nettle is a
witch (she is an herb-wife, which is different).

**Hob.** A scarecrow in the hedge who has started sitting down. Wants a hat. Guards the oak
once he has one.

**Wren.** Seven, in a hat three sizes too big, the most powerful wizard in the wood. Lives in
the Tower with a bear-golem she made at four and a tower that talks. Wants: someone her own age
to play with. Fear: that the tower will not let her out, which it will not. Hands: plays three
chess games at once and loses on purpose when bored. Contradiction: can turn furniture into
butterflies and cannot reach the top shelf.

**The Tower.** Wren's parents, the wood's last two wizards, who bound themselves into the
stones three years ago when the Ember stirred, to keep the wards up. Over-protective because
they are. Talks from everywhere, politely, tired.

**Teddy.** Eleven feet of patched plush with one button eye, Wren's first spell. Does not speak.
Sits on things. A hug puts out small fires.

**Nana Nettle.** The oldest goblin, an herb-wife, small as a loaf, six shawls. Sells at Stall 9.
Her great-great-grandmother sold the dwarves lamp oil and was given the lullaby by the thane's
wife; Nettle has hummed it every night for ninety years without knowing why. Wants somebody to
remember the dwarves. Makes the pie Grizzle claims in chapter 5.

**Brokk Emberward.** Ghost of the last thane, asleep on a stone throne in a crown too big. Stayed
behind to be the Heart's memory. Wants his people remembered and his son's toy horse. Hands:
rings on his beard that he turns when thinking. Opinion: the dragon was always going to be the
one who stayed.

**Sunder.** Bronze and gold, honest, tired. Never lies and never volunteers. Wants to eat a pie
and to get up. Fear: that if she moves, it wakes. Was drawn as a hatchling by Brokk's son; the
drawing is in the nursery.

**Kindle.** The forge-fire. A child, in its way. Asks "who am I." Becomes a hearth-fire the size
of a dog when reminded, and moves into the bakery oven.

**Thane Hild** (chapter 6). Brokk's great-great-granddaughter, a hammer, a baby on her hip,
leader of the dwarves in the hidden valley, who tell the story every night and never forgot.

## Secret

- Kindle is not evil. Every fight with it is a conversation.
- The Heart lights facet by facet with true things about the dwarves: the lamp oil, the toy horse, the nursery drawing, the lullaby, the last words at the gate.
- The brass frame is on the great forge's anvil, kept warm by Kindle for three hundred years.
- Wren's parents can walk out of the tower door at the chapter 5 feast, if the DM wants that beat.
