# Chapter 1: The Notice Board

## Premise
It is a Tuesday, bake-day, and the square is full. The heroes find four jobs on the board with
their names on, in a small hand. A missing cat (who is on the council and on the board). A
scarecrow in the hedge who wants a hat. A goblin who overcharged a widow for mushrooms that
have started glowing. A light under a door in an oak. The cat wrote the notes. The light is a
piece of what sleeps under the mountain, and it has started coming up.

## Goal the party will understand
"Do the jobs on the board and find out who wrote your names."

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Bake-day (social)
> The square smells of bread because the oven in the middle of it is lit, and half the town is
> standing round it with cloths over their bowls, waiting for their turn and talking. There is a
> notice board by the alehouse door. Four notes are pinned to it, and the handwriting is very
> small, and each note has one of your names on it. A fat ginger cat with a blue ribbon is
> sitting on top of the board, looking at you. The baker leans out of her window. "Oh good,
> you're here. Biscuit said you would be."
- **Setup:** Mayor Bramble, flour to the elbows, explains the pies came back. The cat says nothing yet. The kids pick first.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keshu picks a job. Aarya looks at the handwriting and says what she thinks wrote it (a paw).
- **Choices:**
  - 🐱 "Find the councillor's cat" (he is right there; he wants following)
  - 🎩 "The scarecrow wants a hat"
  - 💰 "The goblin overcharged the widow"
  - ✨ "A light under the oak"
- **Outcomes:** The cat gets down, walks toward the hedge, stops, and looks back.
- **Image:** A village square on bake-day, people round a smoking stone oven holding bowls under cloths, a notice board with a ginger cat on it
- **Music:** fiddle from the alehouse

### Scene 2: A hat and a price (social, two short scenes)
> The scarecrow in the hedge is sitting down, which scarecrows do not do. "Crows," it says.
> "They laugh. A scarecrow with no hat is a stick in a shirt."
> Further along, a goblin in a tweed waistcoat is holding a basket of mushrooms at arm's
> length and arguing with an old woman. "Twelve pence." "They were three on Saturday." "On
> Saturday, madam, they were not lit up like that."
- **Setup:** Any hat will do for Hob; a kid's drawing of a hat is accepted and Wren can make it real later. Grizzle is overcharging because the mushrooms frighten him and he wants rid of them. The truth settles it.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keshu gives Hob a hat and describes it (canon). Aarya counts pence and finds the fair price.
- **Choices:**
  - 🎩 Give the scarecrow a hat
  - 🍄 Ask Grizzle why they glow
  - 💰 Pay the widow's price yourself
  - 😠 Threaten the goblin (he faints; the widow is unimpressed)
- **Rolls:** Persuasion Easy.
- **Outcomes:** Hob stands up and offers to watch the oak. Grizzle says the mushrooms lit up "the night the cart came back, like something under the roots rolled over."
- **Image:** A scarecrow sitting in a hedge being handed a paper hat by a child, a goblin in a waistcoat haggling with an old woman nearby
- **Music:** hens, the bell striking

### Scene 3: Knock (exploration)
> At dusk there is light under one part of the hedge. The cat is sitting in front of it with
> his tail going. Behind the hedge an oak so old it is hollow, and set into the hollow a door
> with an iron knocker shaped like a sleeping fox, and light under the door, warm, the colour
> of a coal under ash. "Well," says the cat, in a perfectly ordinary voice. "Knock."
- **Setup:** Biscuit talks and always has. Behind the door is a short root-tunnel into the wood, and in it a dwarven rune-stone, cracked, with ember-light leaking from the crack.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keshu knocks. Aarya reads the rune: a dragon, asleep, with its eyes open.
- **Choices:**
  - 🚪 Knock
  - 🐱 Ask the cat what he wants
  - 🔍 Look at the light first
- **Outcomes:** The rune-stone is one of many seals. This one is cracked. The cat says the dragon can feel it from the mountain.
- **Image:** A hollow oak in a hedge with a fox-shaped iron knocker, warm light under the door, a ginger cat sitting in front of it at dusk
- **Music:** nothing, then one bell

### Scene 4: A lantern with legs (action)
> The wood at night is full of small lights. Mushrooms glowing on the trunks. Fireflies. The
> trees themselves, faintly, like coals. One light is bigger than the others and it is coming
> toward you between the trunks, a ball of ember-light the size of a cat, on legs. It stops in
> front of Keshu and crackles. It seems to want something.
- **Setup:** An **Ember-Wisp** (dm/bestiary.md): a sliver of Kindle, curious, drawn to children. Calm it and it follows as a lantern. Strike it and it splits.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keshu holds out his hand; the wisp lands. Aarya names it (canon).
- **Choices:**
  - 🤲 Hold out your hand
  - 🍄 Offer a glowing mushroom (it eats it; pleased)
  - 🗡️ Hit it
  - 🏃 Run
- **Rolls:** Animals or Nature Easy. No roll for a held-out hand.
- **Outcomes:** The wisp rides on a shoulder. Biscuit: "That's a bit of the Ember. There are more cracks. We need someone who reads dwarven, and the only one is seven and grounded."
- **Image:** A small ball of ember-light with tiny legs landing on a child's hand in a glowing night wood, a ginger cat watching
- **Music:** fireflies, which make no sound

## Complications (roll d6)
1. The crows do laugh. Keshu tells them off and they stop.
2. Grizzle's mushrooms make a hero's nose glow for the session.
3. Mayor Bramble follows the party into the hedge and gets stuck on the stile. Pull him off.
4. The door is locked. The fox knocker wants a riddle ("what has a tail and a door"; a fox, and this oak).
5. The wisp sets a leaf alight. Stamp it. It apologises in sparks.
6. Biscuit falls asleep mid-sentence. He is a cat.

## Rewards
- 100 XP. Level 2. The wisp (companion, 3 HP, light). Hob at the oak. Grizzle's trade token. Council +1, Goblins +1.

## How it ends and what it sets up
The Ember is waking. The seals are cracking. The one person who reads dwarven lives in a tower
that will not let her out until someone beats her at chess.
