# Chapter 3: The Night Market

## Premise
On the dark of the moon the goblins set up forty stalls on a flat stone in the wood that they
think is a nice level place. It is the dwarven gate. The party needs three things: a map that
draws itself (Stall 3), the song that opens the gate (Nana Nettle, Stall 9), and to catch
whoever is stealing from the stalls at midnight, which goblins do not do. It is wisps, carrying
spoons underground "to remember by."

## Goal the party will understand
"Trade for what you need and find the way down."

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Three rules (trading)
> Lanterns hung in trees so big their trunks are walls. Stalls selling mushrooms that glow,
> fog in bottles, maps that draw themselves, and boots that walk back to the stall if you do
> not pay; one pair is walking back now, with nobody in them. Grizzle waves you over. "Friends.
> Three rules. Don't touch what you can't pay for. Don't say 'cheap'. And everything, everything,
> is for trade. What've you got?"
- **Setup:** Each hero has something to trade: a story (ten silver), a drawing (twenty), a song (fifteen), a kid's joke (priceless, once). The map is at Stall 3. Nana Nettle at Stall 9 will teach the gate-song if the party stops whatever is ruining her stall at midnight.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keshu trades a drawing (it stays on the goblin's stall forever). Aarya haggles for the map.
- **Choices:**
  - 🗺️ Trade for the map
  - 🎵 Ask about the gate-song
  - 🍄 Ask Grizzle about the thefts
  - 👢 Try the walking boots (they walk you to Stall 9)
- **Rolls:** Persuasion Tricky, Easy with a story.
- **Outcomes:** Map acquired. Nettle's price: catch her thief.
- **Image:** Children at a goblin stall trading a crayon drawing for a map that is drawing itself, goblins in waistcoats leaning in to see
- **Music:** a hurdy-gurdy

### Scene 2: Spoons (stakeout)
> Midnight. The lanterns go down to a glow. You are crouched behind Stall 9 with Wren and a bag
> of mushrooms. Then, out of the ground, lights: a dozen wisps pushing up through the roots,
> each one taking something small and shiny and pulling it back down. A spoon. A button. A
> goblin's hat, with the goblin still in it.
- **Setup:** Wisps, harmless, collecting shiny things for the Ember. Calm one with the party's own wisp and follow them to the crack in the stone.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keshu's wisp talks to them in crackles; the kids interpret. Aarya pulls the goblin out of his hat.
- **Choices:**
  - 🤲 Calm them with your wisp
  - 🪤 Net one
  - 🏃 Follow them down the roots
  - ✨ Wren makes them sleepy
- **Rolls:** Animals Easy. Athletics Easy for the hat.
- **Outcomes:** A crack in the flat stone with a line of light drawn across it in the shape of a door. It needs the song. Nettle, grateful, sings it and teaches the kids the chorus.
- **Image:** A dozen small ember-wisps coming up through tree roots at midnight carrying spoons and buttons, a goblin being pulled along by his hat
- **Music:** nothing, then a hurdy-gurdy string

### Scene 3: Don't let it remember you (social)
> Nana Nettle is small as a loaf and wrapped in six shawls. "My great-great-granny sold the
> dwarves their lamp oil," she says. "One night they all came up through this gate, every one,
> carrying what they could, and the last one, the thane's wife, gave Granny a song and said,
> 'don't let it remember you.' Then they walked into the wood and the wood moved its paths and
> nobody found them." She sniffs. "I remember them. Somebody should."
- **Setup:** "Don't let it remember you": the Ember learns the shape of what it sees. The gate-song is a lullaby; the Ember sleeps when it is sung.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Learn the chorus (two lines the DM makes up with them; canon). Keshu asks what a dwarf looked like; Nettle draws one on the back of her ledger.
- **Choices:**
  - 🎵 Learn the song
  - ❓ Ask where the dwarves went
  - 🎁 Give Nettle something (Goblins +1)
- **Outcomes:** The song. The rules. The last entry in Nettle's lamp-oil ledger is in dwarven; Wren reads it: "paid in full, and sorry."
- **Image:** A tiny ancient goblin in six shawls teaching two children a song by lantern-light, a drawing of a dwarf on the back of a ledger
- **Music:** the lullaby, hummed

### Scene 4: The gate (action)
> You sing. Badly, mostly, and together. The flat stone the market stands on shivers, and the
> line of light across it opens on stairs going down, and cold old air comes up. Every goblin
> in the market stops talking. And up the stairs, fast, comes something that is not a wisp. A
> dog made of coals, and it has heard the song.
- **Setup:** The **Ember-Hound** (dm/bestiary.md, HP 22). It hates the lullaby; singing it gives it disadvantage; three verses from a child put it to sleep.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Sing the chorus every round. Cannot fail; it gets sleepier.
- **Choices:**
  - 🎵 Sing it down
  - ⚔️ Fight it
  - 🧙 Wren's spell (a bucket of rain)
  - 🧸 Teddy sits on it
- **Outcomes:** It shrinks to a wisp and yawns. The stairs are open. Grizzle hands out lanterns "on credit" and writes it down. Level 4.
- **Image:** A dwarven stone gate grinding open under a goblin market, a dog made of glowing coals coming up the stairs, children singing with their eyes shut
- **Image (after):** A dog made of coals shrunk to a sleepy little wisp yawning on the top step of an open dwarven stairway, goblins in waistcoats handing the heroes lanterns, the heroes grinning
- **Music:** the lullaby, then a yawn

## Complications (roll d6)
1. The walking boots take a hero to the stall that sells second-hand dreams.
2. A goblin child wants to come. His mother says no. He is found asleep in the Halls' nursery in chapter 4.
3. The map draws the dragon and refuses to draw anything else for an hour.
4. Grizzle tries to sell the party their own wisp. Decline.
5. Nettle's shawl is a dwarven cloak. It is warm. She lends it.
6. The lullaby puts Teddy to sleep. Carry him.

## Rewards
- 200 XP. Level 4. The map. The gate-song. Nettle's cloak (resist cold once). Goblins +3.

## How it ends and what it sets up
The stairs go down. The map draws them as you go. Three hundred years empty, and a cup still
on a table somewhere below.
