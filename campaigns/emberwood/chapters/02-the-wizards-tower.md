# Chapter 2: The Wizard's Tower

## Premise
Wren is seven. Wren's parents are the tower, since three years ago, and the tower has not let
her out since. She will read the runes if the heroes play with her properly, and the tower has
rules: no running, no lying, and the stairs go where they like. Getting her out means
convincing two tired parents made of stone that their daughter can go into the wood with
strangers. They are not wrong to worry.

## Goal the party will understand
"Make friends with Wren and get her to read the dwarven writing."

## Scenes

### Scene 1: The stairs are on the second floor today (puzzle)
> The tower leans. Every window is a different colour and a wooden toy horse is drifting out of
> the top one and back in. The door opens before you knock. A voice from everywhere, polite and
> very tired: "Welcome. The young mistress is on the fourth floor. The stairs are on the second
> floor today. Please don't run, and please don't lie. The tower can tell."
- **Setup:** Three rooms: the Library (books fly like starlings; a kid must shush them), the Kitchen (a pot wants a recipe; a kid invents one, canon), the Stairs (which ask each hero one honest question and vanish for a round at a lie).
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keisha shushes the library. Arya gives the pot a recipe.
- **Choices:**
  - 📚 Library first
  - 🍲 Kitchen first
  - 🪜 Find the stairs
- **Rolls:** Honesty passes. A lie loses a round.
- **Outcomes:** The fourth floor: a girl in a hat too big, on a carpet, three chess boards, a bear in the corner so large it has to sit.
- **Image:** A spiral library with books flying like birds and a child with her finger to her lips
- **Music:** a harp, ticking

### Scene 2: I always win (game)
> "I'm Wren," says the girl, "and I always win, and it's boring, so you have to try properly."
> She is playing three games at once against the tower, a teddy bear the size of a shed, and
> herself. "If you win, you can ask me anything. If you lose, you tell me a story. Those are
> the rules. I made them."
- **Setup:** Not real chess. Each game is a contest: d20 + INT for grown-ups, or any game a kid invents on the spot, with rules Wren agrees to instantly. Losing means a story, which is the real win.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keisha invents the game. Arya tells the story.
- **Choices:**
  - ♟️ Chess (INT Tricky)
  - 🎲 The kids' game
  - 📖 Just a story
  - 🧸 Ask the bear for advice (he points)
- **Outcomes:** Wren will read the rune if she is taken outside. The tower says no from every wall at once.
- **Image:** A small girl in a huge pointed hat on a floating carpet among three chess boards, a giant bear playing one of them with a paw
- **Music:** chess pieces clicking

### Scene 3: It's a lock (exploration)
> Wren stands in the root-tunnel in front of the cracked stone and takes her hat off, which
> she has never done in front of anyone. "It's a lock," she says. "It says: the Ember sleeps
> while the Heart remembers. And the Heart is a place. Under the mountain." She puts the hat
> back on. "Something has forgotten the dwarves. That's why the locks are going. Somebody has
> to remember them."
- **Setup:** Exposition made active: the kids ask, Wren answers. The seals are memory. The way under the mountain is through the goblins' market, which stands on the gate.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keisha asks what a dwarf is (Wren is delighted to explain). Arya copies the rune into her journal (advantage in the Halls later).
- **Choices:**
  - ❓ Ask about the Ember
  - ❓ Ask about the dwarves
  - 🏔️ Ask how to get under the mountain
- **Outcomes:** Wren wants to come. The tower will say no. The tower is wrong.
- **Image:** A small girl with her hat off touching a cracked glowing rune-stone in a tunnel of roots, four heroes and a cat behind her
- **Music:** a drip

### Scene 4: Tower, stop being a baby (action)
> "Absolutely not," says the tower from everywhere. The windows slam. The stairs leave. The
> bear stands up to his full height, which turns out to be eleven feet. Wren folds her arms.
> "I'm going. They're my friends. Tower, stop being a baby."
> The tower, which has never been called a baby, throws the furniture.
- **Setup:** The **Tower Tantrum** (dm/bestiary.md): one piece of flying furniture a round, DEX save Easy. Teddy blocks the door and must be reasoned with: he only wants Wren safe; promise him the job of guarding her on the trip and he comes. The front door opens to "please".
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keisha says please to the door. Arya talks to the bear.
- **Choices:**
  - 🧸 Reason with the bear
  - 🙏 Say please to the door
  - 🪑 Dodge and run
  - 🧙 Let Wren handle it (butterflies; the tower sulks)
- **Outcomes:** Wren and Teddy join. The tower passes a packed lunch out of a window without a word. Level 3.
- **Image:** Furniture flying down a tower hallway, a small wizard with folded arms, an eleven-foot teddy bear filling a doorway, a child ducking a chair
- **Music:** a harp played very fast, then a long sigh

## Complications (roll d6)
1. The library books follow a hero home and must be posted back.
2. The kitchen's recipe is now a real dish in Hearth. Name it.
3. Wren turns a hero's hair to feathers for a session.
4. Teddy does not fit through the oak door. Wren shrinks him and he is embarrassed.
5. The tower asks for a postcard. Send one from each world.
6. Biscuit and Wren have met before. They do not get on. By the end of the chapter they are sharing a lunch.

## Rewards
- 150 XP. Level 3. Wren (one spell a scene, kid-chosen). Teddy (HP 20; carries Wren). The rune copy. The Tower +1, grudgingly.

## How it ends and what it sets up
The locks are memory. The way under is through the goblins' market, on the dark of the moon.
