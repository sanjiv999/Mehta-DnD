# Chapter 2: The Wizard's Tower

## Premise
Wren is seven. Wren is the most powerful wizard in the Emberwood. Wren's parents are "away" (a
long story; see dm/secrets.md) and the tower looks after her, badly. She will help read the
runes if the heroes play with her properly, and the tower has rules: no running, no lying, and
the stairs go wherever they want.

## Goal the party will understand
"Make friends with Wren and get her to read the dwarven runes."

## Scenes

### Scene 1: The tower's rules (exploration, puzzle)
> The tower is crooked and every window is a different colour. Toys float out of the top one and
> drift back in. The door opens before you knock. Inside, a voice from everywhere, polite and
> tired: "Welcome. The young mistress is on the fourth floor. The stairs are on the second floor
> today. Please do not run, and please do not lie; the tower can tell."
- **Setup:** The tower is alive and over-protective. Three rooms to pass: the Library (books fly; a kid must shush them), the Kitchen (a pot wants a recipe; a kid invents one), the Stairs (which ask each hero a question and must be answered honestly).
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keisha shushes the library. Arya invents a recipe. Both answer the stairs.
- **Choices:**
  - 📚 Library first
  - 🍲 Kitchen first
  - 🪜 Find the stairs
- **Rolls:** Any lie makes the stairs vanish for a round. Honesty passes automatically.
- **Outcomes:** Fourth floor: a girl in a hat three sizes too big, on a flying carpet, surrounded by chess sets.
- **Image:** A spiral library where books fly like birds, a child with her finger to her lips shushing them

### Scene 2: Chess with Wren (social, game)
> "I'm Wren," says the girl, "and I always win, and it's boring, so you have to try properly."
> She has three chess sets going at once, playing against the tower, a teddy bear, and herself.
> "If you win one game you can ask me anything. If you lose, you have to tell me a story. Those
> are the rules. I made them."
- **Setup:** Not real chess. Each game is a contest: d20 + INT (grown-ups) or a kid's riddle/game of the kid's choosing (Keisha picks the game; Wren agrees to anything; the kids may invent rules). Losing means telling Wren a story, which she loves. Win or lose, Wren warms up.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keisha picks the game. Arya tells the story if anyone loses (this is the real win).
- **Choices:**
  - ♟️ Play chess (INT Tricky)
  - 🎲 Play the kids' game
  - 📖 Just tell her a story
  - 🧸 Ask the teddy bear for advice (it is a golem; it helps)
- **Outcomes:** Wren agrees to read the runes if the heroes take her outside, which the tower forbids. The tower must be persuaded (Persuasion Tricky) or tricked honestly (promise to bring her back by dark; keep it).
- **Image:** A small girl in a huge pointed hat sitting cross-legged on a floating carpet among three chess sets, a teddy bear playing one of them

### Scene 3: Reading the rune (exploration)
> Wren stands in front of the cracked rune-stone in the hedge-oak tunnel and takes her hat off,
> which she has never done in front of anyone. "It's a lock," she says. "It says: the Ember
> sleeps while the Heart remembers. And the Heart is... oh. The Heart is a place. It's under the
> mountain. And it's forgotten." She puts her hat back on. "Something's forgotten the dwarves.
> That's why the locks are breaking. Somebody has to remember them."
- **Setup:** Exposition made active: Wren translates; the kids ask questions; the DM answers through Wren. The seals are tied to memory. The dwarves are forgotten, so the Ember stirs.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keisha asks Wren what a dwarf is (Wren explains gleefully). Arya copies the rune into her journal (gives advantage in the Deep Halls later).
- **Choices:**
  - ❓ Ask about the Ember
  - ❓ Ask about the dwarves
  - 🏔️ Ask how to get under the mountain
- **Outcomes:** The way under the mountain is through the goblins' night market, which sits on an old dwarven gate. Wren wants to come. The tower will say no. The tower is wrong.
- **Image:** A small girl with her hat off touching a cracked glowing rune-stone in a tunnel of roots, four heroes and a cat behind her

### Scene 4: The tower says no (action, chase, comedy)
> "Absolutely not," says the tower, from everywhere. The windows slam. The stairs run away.
> The teddy bear stands up to its full height, which is now eleven feet. Wren folds her arms.
> "I'm going. They're my friends. Tower, stop being a baby."
> The tower, which has never been called a baby, throws a tantrum. Furniture flies.
- **Setup:** Comic chase out of the tower. The **Tower Tantrum** (dm/bestiary.md): flying furniture (one attack per round, DEX save Easy), the bear-golem blocking the door (must be reasoned with: it only wants Wren safe; promise it a role as her guardian on the trip and it comes along), the door that will not open until someone says the magic word ("please", obviously).
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keisha says "please" to the door. Arya talks to the bear.
- **Choices:**
  - 🧸 Reason with the bear
  - 🙏 Say please to the door
  - 🪑 Dodge furniture and run
  - 🧙 Let Wren handle it (she turns the furniture into butterflies; the tower sulks)
- **Outcomes:** Wren and Teddy (the bear-golem) join the party. The tower, sulking, hands Wren a packed lunch through the window. Level 3.
- **Image:** Furniture flying around a tower hallway, a small wizard girl with folded arms, an eleven-foot teddy bear blocking a door, children dodging a flying chair
- **Music:** frantic harp, then a sad little tower sigh

## Complications (roll d6)
1. The library books follow a hero home and must be returned.
2. The kitchen pot's recipe is now a real dish in Hearth. Name it.
3. Wren turns a hero's hair into feathers. It grows out in a session.
4. Teddy will not fit through the oak door. Wren shrinks him to normal size; he is embarrassed.
5. The tower asks for a postcard. Send one.
6. Councillor Biscuit and Wren have met before and do not get on. They do by the end.

## Rewards
- 150 XP. Level 3. Wren (ally; casts one spell per scene, kid-chosen). Teddy (ally; HP 20; carries Wren). The rune copy. The Tower +1 (grudging).

## How it ends and what it sets up
The locks are memory. The dwarves are forgotten. The way under is through the goblins' market.
