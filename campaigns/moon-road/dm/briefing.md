# Lanterns of the Moon Road: DM briefing for Machli and Bolt

DM only. Spoilers throughout. Written for Sanjiv, for the night you switch worlds.

## The story in one paragraph
An old pilgrim road runs from the sea to a mountain shrine. Seventy years ago the Shogun built a
wide new highway in the valley and the travellers left. The stone lanterns on the old road are
how the mountain spirits find their way home each autumn, and this year they are going out, one
station at a time. The cause is Old Keeper Hoshi, alone at the top. At night his loneliness puts
on his straw hat and walks down the road blowing lanterns out, so lost spirits will climb the
mountain looking for light and somebody will come. Hoshi does not know. The heroes are the
somebody.

## Tone
Quiet, odd and gentle. Spirits are polite, and rudeness is the real danger. Paper, salt and
song work on them, and a sword only annoys them. The Hollow Monk is sad, not scary. Every fight
ends with someone being heard.

3. **The crossing.** Peacock Throne's Door, the dry step in the baoli, opens only when the
   throne's wick is recovered in chapter 5, and the kids have not played that far. The bridge I
   recommend: Machli's half-map warms on the dry step, a thin line on it lights up, and a path of
   lantern light runs out over the water. It ends at a red torii standing in a grey sea with no
   shrine behind it. Machli swims through. Bolt, who is waterproof, walks the sea floor beside
   her. Peacock Throne waits for them, and the crack in the Door stays open because of the map.
4. **Local names.** Machli becomes a *same* (a shark spirit) and Bolt a *karakuri* (a clockwork
   doll). I record both under `aliases` when you say go.

## Why these two heroes fit
**Machli.** Her map can show the Moon Road as a thin line, so she knows she is on a road. Out of
the water she breathes fire, which melts the ice-sprites at the lake in one go. Her nose is the
detective in chapter 4, where the clues are straw, sulphur and cold. She is afraid of cliffs,
and chapter 6 has a mountain stair and a tengu's wind. Let her glide. That is the payoff.
**Bolt.** The road is lit by lanterns at night, which suits a solar panel badly and a robot who
fears running down. A rule I suggest: lantern light gives him half a charge, so every relit
lantern is good news for him personally. His Machine Friends and Transform have plenty to
work with: water wheels, gates and a lion-dance costume at the castle. The tengu can notice his
golden feather and bow to it. Kiko climbs onto Keshu's shoulder in scene 1 and stays, so Keshu carries the fox
and the fox's name all road. Aarya's jobs are counting, reading and spotting, so she keeps the
detective's eye.

## Running the fights
- **Shadows** (ch 1, 4): salt makes them flee and light makes them hesitate. They end as a handful of straw.
- **Sprites** (ch 2): pinches and hat-stealing. They end laughing.
- **Ice-sprites** (ch 3): freeze feet. A hug, a song or a warm charm melts one, and so does Machli's fire.
- **Bath monkeys** (ch 4): towel thieves, paid off with a rice cake.
- **The Hollow Monk** (ch 5, 6): 35 HP and cannot be killed. Light, parade and salt together drive him off. The cold breath costs a turn, not hit points. In chapter 6 he ends when the lantern is lit and Hoshi is not alone.
- **The tengu** (ch 6): never fights. He asks a riddle. The answer is a shadow.

The colour trick that beat the glass shapes does not work here. This world's weaknesses are
salt, warmth, light and song. Every fight also has a payoff picture, which Claude reveals when
it is won.

## With a five-year-old at the table
Keep the Monk far away until chapter 5. He never touches anyone. If Keshu is unsure, Kiko
climbs onto his shoulder and says "late, but not lost" and the scene softens. Give Bolt the
first job of every scene. At five, the first job keeps him in the story.

## The thing to remember
Nobody is a villain. Whoever sits with Hoshi at the end wins the game.
