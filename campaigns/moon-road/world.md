# Lanterns of the Moon Road: world bible

Player-safe except the final section. Descriptions are chosen from here.

## The road

The Moon Road is the old pilgrim road from the sea to the shrine on Mount Tsukimi. Seventy
years ago the Shogun built a new highway along the valley floor, wider, with post towns every
few miles and inns licensed by the government, and the traffic went there. The old road kept
its six stations and its stone lanterns and lost almost everyone else. Grass grows between the
paving stones. The honjin inn at the castle town, where lords once slept, is a sake warehouse.

A station is a cluster of houses with deep eaves along one street, a water trough fed by a
bamboo pipe, a shrine with a gate, and a stone lantern on a post at each end of the street.
In autumn every household used to light the lanterns at dusk so the mountain spirits, who come
down to the valleys for summer, could find their way home to the mountain for winter. That is
what the lanterns are for. Most households have forgotten, and light them because their
grandmothers did.

## What people do

Rice in the valleys; buckwheat higher up, eaten as soba noodles cut by hand. Persimmons peeled
and hung on strings under the eaves to dry in autumn, so every house wears an orange curtain.
Miso soup, pickled radish, a grilled fish if the river is kind. Tea at every door, offered before
a name is asked. Pilgrims wear straw sandals that wear out in a day and a half, buy new ones at
each station, and hang the old pair on a shrine fence for luck, so every shrine has a fence of
dead sandals. Wishes are written on small wooden plaques and tied to a rack; the rack clatters
in wind. At the checkpoint on the castle road a traveller shows papers or is turned back.

Hot springs in the mountains: wooden tubs outside under pines, sulphur in the air, snow monkeys
who bathe too and steal towels. A village of inns around a spring is loud with clogs on wet wood.

Tsukimi is the autumn moon-viewing: pampas grass in a jar, round rice dumplings stacked in a
pyramid, everyone on the veranda looking up. The mountain is named for it.

## The spirits

Yokai are everywhere and most are polite. Rudeness is the real danger. Paper, salt and song
work on them; a sword only annoys them. Specific rules the players will learn:

- A **kappa** has a dish of water in its head that is its strength. Bow to it and it must bow
  back, and the water spills, and it is harmless and honest until the dish is refilled. It loves
  cucumbers; parents write their children's names on cucumbers and throw them in the river so
  the kappa leaves the children alone. It keeps its promises exactly.
- A **tanuki** wears a leaf on its head to transform, drums on its belly at night, loves sake,
  and is offended by not being invited. A hundred of them are a parade.
- A **kasa-obake** is an old umbrella that has become a spirit, with one eye and one leg.
- **Ice-sprites, mischief sprites** are what the weather and the woods do when nobody is watching.
- An **oni** is large, horned, strong, and in this story extremely polite. Nothing an oni holds can be blown out.
- A **tengu** lives on the mountain, has wings and a long nose, asks riddles and does not fight children.
- **Kitsune** foxes serve shrines, have one tail per hundred years, and forget themselves if they are away from their shrine too long.
- The **dragon** under Lake Kagami dreams the weather.

## The six stations

1. **Shiokaze**, a fishing village on a grey shore. The torii stands in the sea at the end of a pier and there is no shrine behind it; the shrine is the mountain, and the gate is the start of the road. Fishers, drying racks, a cat that owns the pier.
2. **The Bamboo Sea**, a forest so tall it makes its own dusk, whose paths move unless the tanuki parade is walking them. The tanuki village of Pon-Pon in the middle, built of drums.
3. **Lake Kagami**, a mirror lake under maples, with a lantern on an island. Keeper Yuki's hut on the shore.
4. **Yuzuki Springs**, a hot-spring village of wooden inns stacked up a hillside, steam, clogs, monkeys.
5. **Castle Shirogane**, a white castle over a town with a checkpoint on its road, where the Shogun's wardens keep order and cannot see spirits.
6. **Mount Tsukimi**, a stone stair of a thousand lanterns to one small shrine, a broom, a kettle, a keeper.

## The people (depth)

**Old Keeper Hoshi.** Has lit the first lantern on the mountain every night for seventy years.
His wife swept the shrine and died thirty years ago; the fox stayed another ten, then went down
the road to light the lanterns the villages had stopped lighting and did not come back. The
villages used to send the autumn rice up the mountain. They stopped when the highway opened.
Hoshi is so old he is partly a spirit, and at night his loneliness gets up without him, puts on
his straw hat, and walks down the road blowing lanterns out, so that lost spirits will climb
the mountain looking for light, so that someone will come. He does not know. Wants: someone to
come. Hands: sweeps whether or not there is anything to sweep. Contradiction: the gentlest man
on the road is its villain.

**Kiko / Kikyo.** The shrine's fox. Two tails now (two hundred years), forgot her name when she
was too long from the shrine. Wants: her name, and does not know that is what she wants. Habit:
sits very straight, calls everyone late. Opinion: thinks the tanuki are vulgar and is slightly
jealous of their parade. Letters of her name return one per station.

**Kappa Gen.** Young, apologetic, stole the pier lantern's flame to keep a sick turtle warm.
Wants: to be useful. Bows first, to be safe. His cousin Shin is the plumber at Yuzuki.

**Old Kame.** A sea turtle who carries messages between the sea and the mountain and has done
so for longer than the road has existed. Wants: to deliver this message, from Hoshi, which says
"someone is walking the road the wrong way." Hoshi meant himself and did not know it.

**Boss Ponkichi.** Tanuki, round, a leaf on his head and a sash. Leads the Hundred-Night Parade,
which keeps the forest's paths honest. Wants: an invitation to the Shogun's autumn festival at
the castle. He has never been invited to anything. On strike. Hands: drums his belly when he is
thinking. Opinion: the fox is stuck up.

**Keeper Yuki.** Keeps the lake station alone, sweeps snow off a boat that does not need it.
Wants: the dragon to stop dreaming winter. Practical. Makes a tea that puts you to sleep on purpose.

**Ryūko** and her daughter. The dragon under the lake dreams the weather; her daughter, small and
gold, is the one who speaks. A charm with a hole in it has been whispering "sleep, winter, sleep"
into the lake for a month.

**Okami Tama.** Tanuki innkeeper of the Moonlight Inn at Yuzuki, apron, leaf slipping. Wants:
her guests comfortable and her oni out of custody. Sent for the lantern-lighters by turtle before
anyone else believed the lanterns mattered. Six witnesses will say Daigoro was in the bath.

**Daigoro.** An oni, six feet of red, small horns, a bathrobe, walking the road to apologise to
Hoshi for something his grandfather did (ate Hoshi's kettle, a century ago; Hoshi has forgotten).
Wants: to apologise and be forgiven. Bows seventeen times. Nothing he holds can be blown out.

**Captain Masaru.** Warden of the Shogun's new highway, sent to the old road as a demotion
after an argument with a magistrate. Cannot see spirits. Saw one once as a boy and was told he
was lying. Wants: order, and to see one again. Hands: adjusts his helmet strap when confused,
which is often. Opinion: thinks the lantern-lighters are either mad or spies, and hopes spies.

**Lord Shirogane.** Young, tired, holding a baby who can see spirits and points at them. Wants:
to get through the festival. His grandmother told him about the lanterns; he thought it was a story.

**Kasa.** An umbrella spirit with one eye and one leg, nervous, honest, lives in the corner of
the plumber's shop at Yuzuki.

## Secret

- The Hollow Monk is Hoshi's loneliness. It ends when the first lantern is lit, held, and someone sits with him.
- The charms the Monk drops are Hoshi's own keeper's charms, burned through at the seal because the Monk is only his shadow.
- Kikyo's name: K (station 1), I (2), K (3), O (4), the charm with the whole name (5). Tails: two, three at station 5, four on the mountain.
- The paper shade of the Lantern of Many Roads is inside the first lantern, painted by Kikyo with a fox and a mountain, long ago.
