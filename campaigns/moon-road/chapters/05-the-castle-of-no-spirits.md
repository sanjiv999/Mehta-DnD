# Chapter 5: The Castle of No Spirits

## Premise
The castle is warded. No spirit may enter without invitation, which once was courtesy and is
now a wall. Kiko and Daigoro and the tanuki stop at the gate. The courtyard lantern is out and
the lord does not know what it was for. The heroes must get an invitation written, get into the
castle as people, and light the lantern in front of someone who cannot see any of it. And the
Hollow Monk, for the first time, comes in person.

## Goal the party will understand
"Get the tanuki their card, get inside, and light the courtyard lantern."

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Umbrella sellers (social)
> Banners down every street, food smoke, a lion-dance on a stage with children screaming at
> it, and the castle white above everything. At the first gate Kiko stops dead on your shoulder.
> "Wards. I can't go on. Neither can anyone with more than one tail or fewer than two legs."
> Daigoro bows and sits down to wait. Across the road, a hundred very round umbrella sellers
> are also waiting, in a line, with their leaves showing.
- **Setup:** Masaru is at the checkpoint reading papers. He can get the heroes an audience with Lord Shirogane after the festival's end, if they are not spies, or if they are interesting spies.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keshu chooses a festival mask. Aarya hands Masaru the tanuki's handwritten card and explains it.
- **Choices:**
  - 👮 Ask Masaru for an audience
  - 🎭 Join the lion-dance to get inside (two kids under the lion)
  - 🪁 Fly over the wall (a kid's idea; it nearly works)
  - ☂️ Buy an umbrella from a "seller" (it has a leaf on it)
- **Rolls:** Persuasion Tricky; Easy if Daigoro was freed in chapter 4 and Masaru remembers the bathrobes.
- **Outcomes:** An audience, after the fireworks.
- **Image:** A festival street under a white castle, a lion-dance on a stage, a line of round "umbrella sellers" with leaves on their heads
- **Music:** lion-dance drums, flutes

### Scene 2: The baby (social)
> Lord Shirogane is young and has been in his formal robes since dawn, and he is holding a
> baby who has not. "Lanterns?" he says. "For spirits? My grandmother said that. I thought it
> was a story." The baby reaches toward the window, where nobody but you can see a two-tailed
> fox sitting on the sill. The baby waves. The fox waves back.
- **Setup:** Proof is possible: the weather-charm, the dragon scales, the baby. The lord wants to believe his grandmother.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keshu plays with the baby, who points at every spirit in the room. Aarya uses the weather-charm and makes it snow cherry blossom indoors.
- **Choices:**
  - 🌸 Weather-charm
  - 👶 Let the baby show him
  - 📜 Show him the burned charms
  - 📖 Tell him his grandmother's story back
- **Outcomes:** He writes the invitation himself and puts his seal on it, and asks the heroes to light the lantern "if there is something there to light."
- **Image:** A young lord holding a baby in a castle hall, the baby pointing at a fox on the windowsill that the lord cannot see, cherry blossom falling indoors
- **Music:** one flute

### Scene 3: Little fox (action)
> The invitation is read aloud at the gate and the wards go down like a curtain. A hundred
> tanuki come over the walls. Kiko goes across the courtyard low and fast. And round the
> lantern, as the wards fall, straw hats: a dozen, and one in the middle that is taller than
> the rest and has nearly a face. It looks at the fox. "Little fox," it says, and its voice is like
> wind in dry grass. "Have you remembered your name yet?"
- **Setup:** The **Hollow Monk** in person (dm/bestiary.md, HP 35, cannot be killed, driven off). He knows Kiko. He goes when the lantern is lit and the parade, the drum and the salt ring are all in play.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Keshu leads the tanuki drum. Aarya throws the salt ring. Both cannot fail; they choose the order.
- **Choices:**
  - 🥁 Parade round the lantern
  - 🧂 Salt-charm ring
  - 📜 Lantern-charm
  - 🗡️ Fight him (he is slow and sad and hits hard)
- **Rolls:** Attacks normal. CON save Tricky against his breath, lose a turn.
- **Outcomes:** The lantern lights. The Monk looks at it as if it hurts and walks away up the road, not quickly. He drops a fifth charm. This one is not burned. On it, in a shaking hand, a name: **Kikyo**.
- **Image:** A castle courtyard at night, a tall hollow figure in a straw hat facing a small two-tailed fox, a ring of salt, tanuki coming over the walls
- **Image (after):** A tall hollow figure in a straw hat walking slowly away up a moonlit road, a lit lantern behind it in a castle courtyard, a small two-tailed fox picking up a paper charm with a name on it
- **Music:** fireworks, then one flute

### Scene 4: Kikyo (revelation)
> She looks at the charm for a long time. "Kikyo," she says. "That was my name. He wrote it.
> He gave me charms, when I was the mountain's fox. Before I forgot." She looks up the road.
> "He isn't putting the lanterns out to lose the spirits. He's doing it so they'll go up the
> mountain looking for light. So somebody will finally come."
- **Setup:** The turn. The Monk is the keeper's loneliness. Kikyo was his fox.
- **Jobs for the kids:** Say her name out loud. She gets her third tail in front of them.
- **Choices:**
  - 🏔️ Go to the mountain tonight
  - 🎉 Stay for the festival first (the tanuki insist; level 6 either way)
- **Outcomes:** Fifth ofuda: the name-charm (say a spirit's true name once and it must listen). Level 6.
- **Image:** A fox with three tails holding a paper charm with a name written on it, looking up at a mountain under a full moon
- **Music:** fireworks, far off

## Complications (roll d6)
1. The baby crawls after Kiko. Return him.
2. A tanuki disguised as a warden is given orders and is very bad at them.
3. Fireworks start mid-fight. Everyone has advantage on one roll.
4. Masaru sees a spirit, by accident, and sits down on the ground.
5. The lion-dance lion is a real lion-dog and wants to help.
6. Okami Tama arrives with rice cakes for a hundred tanuki and four heroes.

## Rewards
- 300 XP. Level 6. The name-charm. Castle +3. A lifetime invitation for the tanuki.

## How it ends and what it sets up
The mountain. The shrine. The keeper. The first lantern.
