# Starfall Station: world bible

Player-safe except the final section. The sci-fi is grounded in how people actually live on a
station: the noise, the velcro, the food, the sleeping, the smell.

## The station, as a place to stand in

Starfall is a ring a mile across, spun for gravity, with a hub in the middle where the docks
are. It was built as a repair yard for ships working the nebula and has become a town: five
thousand people, a market on the Ring, a greenhouse deck, a school, a council. The nebula
fills half the sky on the outer side, violet and teal, lit from inside, and the station turns
so the view wheels past every window once a minute.

Things everyone knows about living here. The fans never stop; the whole station sounds like the
inside of a vacuum cleaner and people wear earplugs to sleep. Every surface has velcro on it,
because once an hour, on the hour, the ring stops spinning for ten seconds to rebalance and
everything that is not stuck down floats. Everyone has learned to float politely: you grab a
rail, you keep your tea lidded, you catch what drifts past. Children love it. Bread is not
allowed, because of crumbs; tortillas instead. Food tastes of less up here (fluid shifts, the
nose stuffs up), so hot sauce is currency. Sleeping bags hook to the wall, near a fan, because
without moving air you wake up in a bubble of your own breath. After a spacewalk the airlock
smells of hot metal, like a struck match.

Smells by deck: the Garden Deck is tomato leaves and wet soil; the docks are cold fuel and
ozone; the Ring is noodles and warm electronics; ORBIT's core is nothing at all, which is how
you know you are there.

## What people do

Fix ships. Trade stories, which are the real currency between crews: a docking crew pays for
its berth with the news from wherever it came from. Grow tomatoes under lamps on the Garden
Deck, which was built to feed the station and became its park. Eat noodles at the stall on the
Ring run by a cook who remembers everyone's order. Play catch during the gravity outage. Keep
hobbies, because the station is small; every robot has one, because the robots were built to
copy the people.

## ORBIT

The station's mind. Cheerful, apologetic, runs everything, and a hundred days ago lost a
hundred days. What happened: the Garden Deck's grow-lamps failed and the tomatoes were dying,
and the crew of the *Pakora*, out at the nebula's edge, found a light drifting, a fragment of
something, and jarred it and brought it home to power the lamps. ORBIT shut down her memory
core to divert power to the greenhouse while they wired it in, and when she came back the crew
was gone from her records and so was the reason. The tomatoes lived. The crew lived in a corridor
that was not on the plans and told her who they were every day until they forgot the station too.

## The nebula

Alive. A cloud-whale the size of a city and her child, who was separated from her in a storm
and whose light the crew found drifting and took. The child sings for its light. The mother
answers. Humans who hear both halves try to complete the song and cannot stop: the Choir. Answer
the song, even badly, and a singer wakes up. The storms are the mother's worry.

## The people (depth)

**ORBIT.** Wants to remember; then, for her crew to come home. Fear: that she is the station and
the station was wrong. Voice: "You're... hm. Definitely someone!" Contradiction: runs five
thousand lives and cannot say what happened last spring. Sends lunch to the wrong bay.

**Dockmaster Oola.** An octopus in a bubble suit with eight tool belts, who runs the docks and
turns red when angry, which octopuses do. Wants her ships and crews back. Opinion: the council
are cowards. Met the heroes a hundred days ago and likes them and cannot say why, which annoys her.

**Chair Vexley.** Tall, grey coat, clipboard, posture like a mast. Lost a crew to the cloud two
years ago and signed the letters. Wants nobody else lost. His daughter drew the nebula from the
school window on the last page of his clipboard and he does not know the Whale will show it to
him. Habit: straightens the clipboard. Contradiction: the obstacle, and the only council member
who ever reads the casualty lists.

**Captain Ilse Varga.** First humming captain. Worn flight jacket, kind eyes, a smile she does
not know she has. Happiest she has ever been and knows something is wrong. When a child hums
back she cries once and stops humming for a minute.

**The *Pakora*.** Round, dented, built by the crew from salvage; her engine is keyed to their
keepsakes. Gruff and fond. Will not fly on Tuesdays (nobody knows why; she does). Makes tea if
you press the red button. Remembers the crew when ORBIT could not and has waited a hundred days.

**The Hobby Club.** KNIT-9, a welding bot who knits (built Airlock 7, forgot where; the scarf is the
map). SONNET, a security bot who writes bad sincere poetry; guards sit down to listen. PIP, a
maintenance bot who paints the nebula over and over, and in each painting the thing inside is
closer. TUBA, a cargo bot who plays the tuba badly and loudly and once, at the end, in tune.
They remember the crew. They were asked to keep quiet by a station that was ashamed.

**Captain Tomas** (chapter 3). Young captain of the *Lantern Jack*, drifting in the cloud with
six crew holding hands in a circle, humming. Lucid when a child hums. Proposes to the *Pakora*.

**The Whale and the Child.** The mother, violet, lit in slow pulses, an eye the size of a dock.
The child, as long as the dock, dim. Not enemies at any point.

## Secret

- The crew (the heroes) took the child's light to save the tomatoes. The note in Bay 7 is theirs.
- Airlock 7 is between hatches 6 and 8; KNIT-9's scarf is the map; the wall opens to the hummed tune.
- The jar in the corridor is the child's light and the Lantern's light. Returned in chapter 5; the Whale gives a fragment of her own in exchange.
- Vexley's daughter's drawing is the last page of his clipboard.
